ENDINGS

THE MOON

The Girl Who Returned To The Moon

The eclipse shatters as Bathala reaches Mayari, and instead of fading, the silver cracks across her body begin to dissolve like mist under dawn light; she falls into his arms, breathing again as the world slowly restores the moon above them, no longer broken or dying, while Mayari looks up in quiet disbelief and whispers that she can finally feel “real” for the first time, as if the moon has stopped calling her back.

THE HERMIT REVERSED

When The Moon Forgot Her Name

The world gives in to the eclipse as Mayari’s body fractures into silver fragments that drift upward like ash, her voice breaking as she tells Bathala she was never meant to stay, and even as he reaches for her, she disappears completely into the collapsing sky, leaving only silence behind while the moon vanishes entirely and the night loses all light, as if she never existed at all.

THE SUN

The Sunrise After the Storm

Bathala continues encouraging Hanan despite the repeating mornings and collapsing timelines consuming the city, refusing to give up on the idea that people still deserve new beginnings. During the final frozen dawn, Bathala stays beside her instead of surrendering to Bakunawa’s endless night, allowing the sunrise to finally move again across Manila. Time resumes, the endless repetition ends, and humanity regains the ability to move forward. In the ending scene, Hanan opens a small breakfast café called “Tomorrow Morning,” where customers leave handwritten wishes and future plans taped across the walls.

THE WORLD REVERSED

The Day That Never Arrived

Bathala repeatedly sides with Bakunawa’s belief that humanity endlessly repeats its mistakes and eventually allows the sunrise to disappear completely during the final collapse of dawn. The world becomes trapped in permanent pre-morning darkness while every clock in Manila freezes forever at 6:14 AM. Hanan slowly loses the optimism that once kept her moving forward as people become unable to hope for tomorrow anymore. In the ending scene, her empty café remains frozen in silent.

DEATH

The Weight Shared Between Two People

Bathala continues staying beside Sidapa even after learning he can see everyone’s deaths, helping him slowly accept that temporary things still matter despite their endings. During the final collapse of time, Bathala rejects Bakunawa’s promise of a world without pain and chooses Sidapa instead. The frozen city begins moving again, the eclipse breaks apart, and the silver countdowns above humanity stabilize once more. In the ending scene, Sidapa quietly shares coffee with Libulan on a convenience store rooftop at dawn while ignoring the countdowns above the city for the very first time.

THE HANGED MAN REVERSED

The World Without Tomorrow

Bathala repeatedly agrees with Bakunawa’s belief that endings only create suffering and eventually allows time itself to stop permanently during the eclipse. The countdowns above humanity disappear forever, leaving the city trapped in motionless stillness where nobody dies, changes, or truly lives anymore. Libulan’s music fades into silence while Sidapa remains alone beneath the frozen moon, endlessly wandering unmoving streets where rain hangs motionless in the air. In the final scene, every clock in Manila remains stuck at the exact same second forever.

THE FOOL

The Song Before Dawn

Bathala continues choosing Libulan despite the chaos surrounding him, refusing to see him as dangerous even after learning the truth about his powers. During the eclipse, Bathala reaches for Libulan instead of letting him disappear alone beneath Bakunawa’s silence. Together, they restore the emotions frozen across Manila, causing the eclipse to collapse and the city to breathe again. Libulan survives with weakened powers, and in the final scene, he performs a quiet rooftop set at sunrise while Sidapa watches nearby without looking at anyone’s lifespan for the first time in years.

THE STAR REVERSED

The City Without Heartbeats

Bathala repeatedly distances themselves from Libulan and slowly begins believing Bakunawa’s claim that emotions only cause suffering. During the final eclipse, Bathala allows Libulan’s music to disappear completely, letting silence consume the city. Humanity survives, but people gradually lose the ability to genuinely feel joy, grief, love, or hope. Libulan dissolves into silver light beneath the eclipsed moon, and in the ending scene, Manila continues functioning normally—except nobody smiles, cries, or truly connects with anyone anymore.
