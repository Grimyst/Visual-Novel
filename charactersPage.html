CHARACTERS

Mayari

— The Light That Refuses to Fade

Mayari moves through the world like a quiet exposure of film—unhurried, precise, and always catching what others miss between moments. A photography student by title, but more like a translator of silence, she captures light not as scenery but as memory trying to survive time. Her calm demeanor feels almost lunar itself: steady, distant, softly reflective, yet never empty. She speaks with careful honesty, valuing truth even when it cuts, because falsehood feels heavier to her than pain. The moon is not just something she observes—it feels like an old echo embedded in her bloodstream, surfacing in fragmented dreams of silver oceans, fractured skies, and a presence calling her name from somewhere beyond human language. Though she tries to ground herself in logic and daily life, there is always a subtle gravitational pull in her—an unspoken suspicion that she is not entirely anchored to Earth, but temporarily borrowed by it.

Sidapa

— The Weight of Endings

Sidapa exists in the quiet architecture of finality, a man who reads endings the way others read clocks. At the hospital, he is composed to the point of stillness—movements measured, voice subdued, presence almost ceremonial. To him, life is not chaos but a sequence of inevitable conclusions unfolding in correct order. He perceives lifespans as glowing threads above people’s heads, thinning or fraying with merciless clarity, and this sight has shaped him into someone who never reaches for what time has already marked as lost. Emotion, for Sidapa, is not absence but containment; he feels deeply, but behind sealed doors of restraint built over years of witnessing goodbye after goodbye. Beneath his logic lies a quiet sorrow: the exhaustion of knowing every bond is pre-written as temporary, and the burden of never being allowed to forget that all warmth is scheduled to end.

Libulan

— The Feeling That Can't Be Controlled

Libulan is a living frequency disguised as a man, a DJ who doesn't just play music but reshapes the emotional weather of entire rooms. His presence is kinetic—smiles sharpened like neon, laughter timed like percussion, confidence spilling out in waves that pull people into rhythms they didn’t know they were capable of feeling. On stage, he becomes a conductor of invisible tides, bending joy, longing, and euphoria as if emotion itself were vinyl beneath his fingertips. Yet offstage, the sound collapses. Without an audience to echo against, he becomes strangely muted, as though his own emotions require amplification to exist at all. Hanan

— The Beginning That Never Comes

Hanan is dawn in human form—bright, restless, and perpetually leaning toward tomorrow as if it is always just one more step away. She lives in rhythms: early mornings, repeated habits, clean starts, and the comforting illusion that life can be reset through discipline and intention. Her optimism is not shallow; it is deliberate, forged from the belief that beginnings are not granted but created, even in broken places. She notices emotional shifts with gentle accuracy, often responding not with grand solutions but with small acts of presence that feel like warmth without weight. Yet beneath her radiant persistence lies a quiet paradox: she is endlessly arriving at the threshold of change without ever fully crossing it. Her life feels like a sunrise that never completes its ascent—always glowing, always promising, but suspended in that fragile moment just before everything truly starts.
